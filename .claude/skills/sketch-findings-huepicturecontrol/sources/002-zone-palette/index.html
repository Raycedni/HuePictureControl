A · Golden-Angle HSL (hue = i × 137.508° mod 360, fixed s/l)

Maximum hue separation between adjacent indexes. Saturation pinned at 60%, lightness at 60% — bright enough for the dark surface, muted enough not to clash with the amber brand accent. This was the formula CONTEXT.md "Specifics" listed as the natural option.

What to look for
  • Every adjacent pair is maximally distinct in hue — easy to pick out at a glance even at 6+ channels.
  • Chips in LightPanel match strip zones 1:1 by index (per-device numbering).
  • Risk: full rainbow can fight the amber brand. Channel 1 = red, which collides emotionally with "stop"/destructive.
  • Risk: same index gives same color across devices — Top/Mid/Bot on Lamp Strip use the same colors as Ch 1/2/3 on TV. CONTEXT.md doesn't say per-device colors must differ, but it could surprise users when colors repeat across the LightPanel.
  • Formula: hsl((i * 137.508) % 360, 60%, 60%). Deterministic, no state.

B · Amber Tonal Ramp (hue locked at 38°, lightness + saturation step)

Stays inside the brand. Channels read as members of the same family rather than independent colors. Distinguished by lightness (40 → 75) and saturation modulation, so the eye sorts them by "darker → brighter" instead of by hue.

What to look for
  • Feels brand-aligned and cohesive — channels read as "siblings".
  • Past ~5 adjacent channels the differences become subtle. Two adjacent zones at indexes 4 and 5 are still distinguishable but borderline.
  • Risk: at higher channel counts (TV Back's 6 channels), the ramp runs out of headroom — Ch 5 and Ch 6 are close.
  • Formula: hsl(38 - (i * 3), 90 - (i * 6), 45 + (i * 6)) roughly. Bounded; rolls toward warm pastels.

C · Two-Family Alternation (warm/cool, anchored to brand)

Indexes alternate between a warm family (amber, orange, gold) and a cool family (slate-blue, teal, periwinkle). Adjacent zones are always in opposing families so contrast is guaranteed even at high channel counts. Brand still dominates because the warm family uses the existing palette anchors.

What to look for
  • Adjacent contrast is guaranteed (warm/cool always alternates).
  • Brand amber is index 0, so first channel everywhere is the brand color — feels intentional rather than arbitrary.
  • Risk: a "two-family" rule is less mathematically clean than A or B; either someone hand-curates 5–10 colors per family, or you generate them.
  • Cooler counterpoint is muted (no neon) to feel native to the dark+amber surface.
  • Test case: stress-test fixture (6 channels) reads cleanly; Channel 1 = amber and "primary" matches LightPanel chip + strip + brand identity.
